# Deck design rules

Every slide follows these rules. Check a slide against them before committing it.

## Layout

1. **One idea per slide.** The title says the idea; everything on the slide supports it.
2. **A grid.** Everything aligns to columns and a shared baseline. Same-kind items have the same size and the same spacing.
3. **Balance.** The content fills the slide: no empty half, no crammed corner. White space is deliberate and even.
4. **No orphans.** Nothing floats unattached. If items are not connected, show them as a list or a grid, not as a diagram.
5. **A diagram only shows connections.** Use a diagram when the links matter; use a grid of blocks when you explain what things are (a legend).

## Text

6. **Readable from the back of the room.** At the 1280 px canvas: titles ≥ 40 px, body ≥ 20 px, labels inside blocks ≥ 16 px, nothing under 14 px. If text inside an image would be smaller, change the image, not the font of the rest.
7. **Contrast.** Dark text on light, white text on saturated fills. No grey-on-colour descriptions.
8. **Short.** Phrases, not paragraphs. At most 6 bullets per column.

## Diagrams and blocks

9. **The same visual language everywhere.** A block on a slide looks exactly like the block of that kind in the diagrams: colour, shape, corner radius, title style.
10. **Big enough to read.** A diagram on a slide is cropped or split so labels stay readable; if it does not fit, show a part and link the interactive view.
11. **Standard terms and a legend.** Stage names follow industry terms; colours and shapes are explained once, on the legend slide.

## Process

12. **Check in the browser** at the real slide size before committing. Look for: uneven spacing, misalignment, overflow, tiny text, empty areas.
