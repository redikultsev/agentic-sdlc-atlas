# X7. Measurement & evidence

Phase F1 broad sweep, 2026-11. Not yet verified in depth: `search-snippet` rows were not opened.

## State of the art

As of late 2026 the evidence splits into two layers that disagree. Benchmarks have saturated and been discredited. OpenAI stopped reporting SWE-bench Verified in Feb 2026 because of contamination and flawed tests. In Jul 2026 it retracted its own SWE-bench Pro recommendation after estimating that about 30% of Pro tasks are broken. METR found that about half of test-passing Verified PRs would not be merged by maintainers, and that maintainer-graded time horizons are about 7x shorter than grader-scored ones. Field telemetry is a second layer, and it converges on one finding: AI and agents sharply raise activity (commits, PRs, lines of code). Shipped output barely moves, because review becomes the bottleneck. Evidence: NBER 500k devs, +240% commits but +30% releases. Harvard/Jellyfish 718 firms, +30% code, flat resolved issues, +49% review time. A natural-experiment arXiv paper found that switching to a higher-scoring model did not raise merge rates. The quality layer is contested and mostly vendor self-report. Faros (incidents per PR +243%) and GitClear (duplication +81%, refactoring -70%) say quality is worse. Greptile says agent PR revert, churn and P0 rates are at or below human levels. METR's RCT line ended in a design failure: selection effects made the 2026 follow-up uninterpretable. Self-report surveys remain unreliable. So no one has a trustworthy measure of 'human time per merged, surviving change' yet.

## Approaches

| Approach | What | Evidence | Seen in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Execution-graded issue benchmarks** | Real GitHub issues and PRs turned into tasks graded by hidden tests (SWE-bench family). Cheap and reproducible, but vulnerable to contamination, too-narrow or too-wide tests, and low test coverage. | strong | OpenAI: Why SWE-bench Verified no longer measures frontier coding, OpenAI: Separating signal from noise (SWE-Bench Pro audit), SWE-Bench Pro (Scale AI), SWE-Bench Pro is even worse (Gabor audit) |
| **Continuously refreshed / decontaminated benchmarks** | Mine fresh tasks after model cutoffs so they cannot be in training data (live leaderboards). | mixed | SWE-rebench (Nebius), SWE-bench-Live |
| **Contamination and memorization probes** | Test whether models reproduce gold patches, file paths or problem text from a task ID alone. Elicitation can be red-teamed or done with controlled repo perturbations. | strong | The SWE-Bench Illusion (arXiv 2506.12286), Does SWE-Bench-Verified Test Agent Ability or Model Memory? (arXiv 2512.10218), OpenAI: Why SWE-bench Verified no longer measures frontier coding |
| **Holistic / maintainer-review grading** | Replace or supplement test pass/fail with blinded maintainer merge decisions, normalized by a golden-patch baseline. | strong | METR: Many SWE-bench-Passing PRs Would Not Be Merged, METR: Algorithmic vs Holistic Evaluation |
| **Time-horizon capability metric** | Express agent capability as the human-expert duration of tasks it solves at 50%/80% reliability, then track the doubling time. | mixed | METR Time Horizons (TH 1.0/1.1), On the estimation and validity of AI time horizons (arXiv 2610.12466) |
| **Economic-value benchmarks** | Tasks priced by real freelance payouts, so scores read as dollars earned. | weak | SWE-Lancer (OpenAI) |
| **Randomized controlled trials of developers** | Randomize tasks or developers into AI-allowed and AI-disallowed arms and measure completion time. | mixed | METR early-2025 RCT (19-20% slowdown), METR 2026 uplift update (design abandoned), Google enterprise RCT (21% faster) |
| **Quasi-experimental field telemetry** | Difference-in-differences or event-study designs on large commit, PR, Jira and deploy datasets that compare adopters with non-adopters, or model switches. They measure the attenuation from activity to shipped output. | strong | NBER w35275 Writing Code vs Shipping Code, Harvard Chen & Stratton: AI in the Firm, bottlenecks, Higher-Scoring Coding Models Did Not Get More Work Done (arXiv 2611.23216) |
| **Vendor platform telemetry on quality and delivery** | Engineering-analytics and code-review vendors report churn, duplication, revert, incident and review-time trends from customer data. | mixed | Faros AI Engineering Report 2026 (Acceleration Whiplash), GitClear Maintainability Gap 2026, Greptile: Rise of the Overnight Agents, CodeRabbit AI vs Human Code report, GitHub Octoverse 2026 |
| **Agentic-PR mining datasets** | Public datasets of PRs authored by agents (identified by bot author, Co-Authored-By trailer or branch prefix) to study acceptance and reverts. | mixed | AIDev dataset (arXiv 2602.09185), Empirical study on reverted changes by AI coding agents (ACM) |
| **Survey-based org capability models** | Large surveys that link AI adoption to DORA-style throughput and instability metrics and to organizational capabilities. | weak | DORA 2025 State of AI-assisted Software Development, METR early-2026 self-reported impact survey, DX AI Measurement Framework |

## Open gaps

- No one measures human review minutes per merged-and-surviving change for parallel agents. METR explicitly says per-task time is ill-defined when developers run several agents concurrently.
- Telemetry studies are flatly contradictory on whether agent code is worse. Faros (incidents per PR +243%) and GitClear (duplication +81%) disagree with Greptile (agent revert and churn at or below human). There is no reconciliation study using the same data and definitions.
- After the retractions of SWE-bench Verified and Pro there is no trusted, uncontaminated, maintained public coding benchmark. OpenAI promised private evals but has published none.
- Line-level survival (what fraction of agent-written lines remain after 3 months) is called the gold standard and is rarely reported.
- No solo-developer or small-scale method is validated. All field evidence is org-level (Jira, PR queues) or open-source.
- Attribution of agent-authored code depends on Co-Authored-By trailers and branch prefixes, so detection is lossy and the 'human' baseline is contaminated by AI-assisted code.
- There is no causal evidence on which harness, review or verification practices close the gap from activity to shipped output. Studies stop at 'review is the bottleneck'.
