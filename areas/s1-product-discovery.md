# S1. Product discovery

Phase F1 broad sweep, 2026-11. Not yet verified in depth: `search-snippet` rows were not opened.

## State of the art

As of late 2026, agents handle the evidence-gathering half of product discovery well: they mine feedback, interviews, tickets and analytics, normalize and deduplicate problems, cite their sources and draft specs. Choosing what to build is still left to humans almost everywhere. The strongest primary evidence is company engineering posts about internal pipelines (Brex Little Big Details, Amplitude Wave, Postman Aira). They share one design: several narrow agents (mine, synthesize/filter, act), strict evidence rules (multi-source, citations, a check against the codebase), and a human review gate. All of their numbers are self-reports. Research on synthetic users and digital twins agrees on one point. Simulated respondents roughly match population-level distributions but fail for individuals (correlation about 0.2 in the Columbia mega-study) and suffer mode collapse (NN/g 2026: 12 labels from synthetic respondents vs 50+ from humans). Agent-generated ideas look novel but get worse once someone executes them (Si et al., ICLR 2026). Autonomous business operators like Project Vend made basic commercial mistakes. The open dispute is whether 'deciding what to build' can be automated at all: vendors such as Amplitude and Polsia claim closed loops, while Cagan, NN/g and the HCI critics argue that judgment and contact with real users cannot be replaced.

## Approaches

| Approach | What | Evidence | Seen in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Feedback-mining pipeline with strict evidence filter** | Narrow agents connected via MCP to feedback sources (tickets, Slack, call transcripts, research repos) mine candidate problems. They strip PII, normalize each to a canonical problem with a dedup fingerprint, then filter by hard criteria (at least two independent sources, still present in the code, small scope). Output is a ranked backlog item or a draft PR, with a human gate before merge. | mixed | Brex Little Big Details, Amplitude Wave, Postman Aira, Linear Product Intelligence, GitHub customer-feedback AI, Dovetail MCP server, David Kelly prioritization MCP |
| **Interview synthesis to opportunity tree with verbatim citations** | The user uploads interview recordings or transcripts. The tool extracts needs and pain points, backs each card with a verbatim quote, and assembles an Opportunity Solution Tree or opportunity score. Humans still run the interviews. | weak | Vistaly AI Product Discovery, Buildworthy, Hypervalid, ProductSense, Aha! Discovery AI assistant, Bifocal |
| **Synthetic users / digital twins for concept testing** | LLM personas, optionally grounded in real interviews or survey histories, answer concept tests, surveys or purchase-intent questions in place of recruited people. | mixed | Synthetic Users platform (tested by NN/g), Generative Agent Simulations of 1,000 People, Interview-Informed Generative Agents for Product Discovery (Adobe, CHI 2026), Columbia Mega-Study of Digital Twins, PyMC Labs Semantic Similarity Rating, Using LLMs for Market Research (Brand, Israeli, Ngwe), Lost in Simulation |
| **Agent desk-research idea validation (GO/PIVOT/KILL reports)** | Coding-agent skills or LangGraph apps run parallel web-research waves (market, competitors, pricing, TAM/SAM/SOM) and return a verdict report. None of these validates its verdicts against real outcomes. | weak | startup-skill, Deep Competitive Analyst, idea-sieve / VettIQ / octomind launch:validate (long tail) |
| **LLM scoring of ideas and startups** | LLMs or multi-agent debates rate ideas or predict startup success, sometimes compiled into interpretable rules. The key negative result: judgments made before execution do not predict results after execution. | mixed | The Ideation-Execution Gap (Si, Hashimoto, Yang), Vela Research startup-prediction line (SSFF, LLM-AR, Policy Induction, RRF), DIALECTIC multi-agent startup evaluation, Kleinert & Urbig 2026 startup evaluations |
| **Evidence-to-spec handoff to coding agents** | A PRD or spec is built from linked customer evidence and served to coding agents (often over MCP) as the source of truth. Includes research on generating requirements and SRS documents. | weak | Productboard Spark, ChatPRD MCP, Amplitude Wave, ReqInOne / Agent4RE / OpenRE-Bench |
| **Autonomous business operator** | An agent owns the full commercial loop (what to sell or build, pricing, outreach) with minimal human input. | weak | Project Vend (Anthropic / Andon Labs), Polsia |
| **Judgment-stays-human position** | Practitioner and HCI arguments that discovery depends on product sense, testing with real users and value/viability judgment. In this view AI supplies inputs but does not make the decision. | mixed | Marty Cagan: AI and the Future of the Product Role, The Synthetic Persona Fallacy (ACM Interactions), NN/g synthetic users articles |

## Open gaps

- No one measures whether agent-surfaced opportunities lead to better business outcomes (retention, revenue) than human-chosen ones. Amplitude's 5% to 70% figure is internal rater approval, not impact.
- No public benchmark for product discovery itself, such as ranking opportunities from a fixed evidence corpus against known outcomes. RE benchmarks start after the needs are known.
- The 'decide' step is barely documented: how agents weigh frequency against severity against strategy, and how often humans overrule them.
- Small-scale or solo settings: almost all evidence comes from companies with large feedback volume. It is unclear what works with a handful of users or with no users at all, as in pre-launch personal projects.
- Sycophancy toward the loudest requester (seen in Project Vend) has not been studied in feedback-mining pipelines.
- Whether idea-validation skills (GO/KILL reports) have ever been checked retrospectively against what actually happened to the ideas.
