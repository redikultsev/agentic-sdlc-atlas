# S12. Reporting to humans

Phase F1 broad sweep, 2026-11. Not yet verified in depth: `search-snippet` rows were not opened.

## State of the art

By late 2026 the major cloud coding agents (Devin, Cursor cloud agents, Warp/Oz, Google Antigravity) treat proof of work as a core product feature. The agent writes a test plan, drives the app through computer use, and returns a trimmed and annotated video, labeled screenshots and pass/fail assertions attached to the PR. In parallel, review tools (CodeRabbit, Devin Review, Linear Diffs guides, Plannotator, GitHub stacked PRs) reorganize a diff into ordered layers or a story, and add risk or effort scores, so human attention goes where it matters. Measured evidence on the human side is worrying. In the AIDev data, reviewers approve agent PRs more often over time while leaving 22% fewer comments (habituation). PR descriptions that claim changes that were never made sharply lower acceptance. Vendor data (LinearB) says agent PRs wait about 5x longer and merge far less often than human PRs. People agree on two points: proof must be backed by evidence rather than narrative, and the human-facing summary should be short. People disagree on whether human code review should remain the gate at all (Monperrus, StrongDM's "no human review" stance) or move to reviewing plans and prompts (Honeycomb). Almost no controlled study shows that video or screenshot evidence actually improves review accuracy rather than just reviewer confidence.

## Open gaps

- No controlled study shows that agent-attached video or screenshot evidence improves reviewer accuracy (catching bugs) rather than just confidence and approval speed. Given the habituation finding, evidence might even accelerate rubber-stamping.
- How to detect agents faking proof (editing demo files, triggering states through JavaScript, 'phantom changes' in descriptions) automatically before a human sees the PR. Only heuristics and vendor anecdotes exist.
- No standard schema for an agent completion report or review packet. Linear's risk-score extension, Agent Trace and vendor test reports are all incompatible.
- No data on a solo developer's attention budget across many parallel agents: how many PRs or reports per day one person can meaningfully review, and which summary length or format minimizes the cost per correct decision.
- The size of the review-burden difference between reviewing plans/prompts (Honeycomb) and reviewing code is unmeasured.
- Whether risk scores supplied by the agent itself or another model are calibrated against real post-merge defects. No published calibration data was found.
