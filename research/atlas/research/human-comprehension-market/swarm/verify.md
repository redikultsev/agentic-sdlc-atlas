# Fact-check of 20 claims (checked 2026-11-21 against primary sources)

Sources: full PDFs for 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11, 12, 14, 16, 17, 18, 19 (curl + pdftotext); PMC full text for 20; figshare PDF via fetchira read for 10; full PDF text via fetchira search results for 4 (LaToza's own PDF); publisher abstract + author preprint text for 13 and 15. Venues cross-checked on arXiv comments, ACM DL, IEEE CSDL and Springer.

| # | Verdict | What the source says (exact text) | Year / venue |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Wettel CodeCity | CONFIRMED | "involving 41 participants from both academia and industry"; "our approach leads to an improvement, in both correctness (+24%) and completion time (-12%), over the state-of-the-practice exploration tools. This result is statistically significant."; "in the case of focused tasks, i.e., tasks which require very precise answers, CodeCity did not perform better than Eclipse+Excel." | ICSE '11, Honolulu: confirmed |
| 2 Code Bubbles | CONFIRMED | "We recruited 20 graduate and 3rd and 4th-year undergraduate students"; "CB users completed both tasks 33.2% faster than EC users"; "CB users were able to significantly finish more tasks overall"; "CB users (Mean: 3.5 min, SD: 1.6) spent significantly less time than EC users (Mean: 11.6 min, SD: 3.08) navigating". Note: task 2 alone was not significantly faster. | CHI 2010, Atlanta: confirmed |
| 3 LACY | CONFIRMED (with nuance) | "We recruited seven participants from Beko: two domain experts and five developers onboarding"; "Learners using expert-guided tours achieved 83% quiz scores versus 57% for AI-only tours"; "Participants completed guided tours approximately 10 minutes faster (∼25 vs ∼35 min)". Nuance: 7 participants in total, 5 of them learners. | arXiv comment: "Accepted for publication at ACM FSE 2026 Industry Track" (Industry Track, not the main track) |
| 4 REACHER | CONFIRMED | Abstract: "12 participants answered control flow questions. Compared to an existing IDE, participants with REACHER were over 5 times more successful in significantly less time." Results: "5.6 times more successfully with REACHER (78%) than with Eclipse alone (14%)"; mean time 11.1 vs 7.2 min; significantly faster on tasks 1, 2, 4, 6 but not on 3 or 5. | VL/HCC 2011, pp. 117-124, DOI 10.1109/VLHCC.2011.6070388: confirmed |
| 5 Tufano et al. | CONFIRMED | "controlled experiment with 29 professional developers" (28 currently professional, 1 former); "Reviewers tend to focus on the code locations indicated by the LLM"; "identified a higher number of low-severity issues while, however, not identifying more high-severity issues"; "did not result in saved time and did not increase the reviewers' confidence." | ICSE '25, pp. 1640-1652, DOI 10.1109/ICSE55347.2025.00060: confirmed (the arXiv page itself gives no venue) |
| 6 MetaMateCR (Meta) | CONFIRMED | "randomized controlled safety trials"; "in a safety trial that compares no AI patches with AI patch suggestions, we see a large regression with reviewers taking over 5% longer to conduct reviews. After investigation, we modify the UX to only show authors the AI patches, and see no regressions in the time for reviews." | arXiv 2507.13499, July 2025; no venue stated (the PDF uses a TSE template header with no volume) |
| 7 Kaufman & Brun, assertions | CONFIRMED | "86 Python programmers"; "correct assertions (74% accuracy) ... incorrect assertions (49% accuracy)"; "natural-language explanations of assertions provided no overall benefit"; "low-quality explanations could impair specification assessment accuracy (p = 0.037, OR = 0.58) while simultaneously increasing developer confidence (p = 0.005, 3.99/5 vs. 4.25/5)". | arXiv 2607.08885; no venue stated |
| 8 Baum et al. | CONFIRMED | "confirmatory experiment with 50 participants, mostly professional software developers"; "We also confirm that the effectiveness of reviews is significantly larger for small code changes. We cannot conclude reliably whether the order of presenting the code change parts influences the efficiency of code review." | Empirical Software Engineering 24(4):1762-1798, 2019 (online 2 Jan 2019): confirmed |
| 9 Fregnan et al. | CONFIRMED | "Recruiting 106 participants ... For one type of defect, participants are not affected by its file's position; for the other, they have 64% lower odds to identify it when its file is last as opposed to first." | ESEC/FSE '22, Singapore: confirmed |
| 10 Codebase chatbots vs tutorials | CONFIRMED (with nuance) | "between-subject experiment with 15 experienced programmers"; "one person dropped out in the middle and did not return (P1)"; tutorial group "spent significantly less time than the Cody group ... in getting started with the codebase (p≈0.0034)"; "programmers assisted by LLM chatbots were more confused and less confident". Nuances: first-typing time was measured after the tutorial, which took an average of 27.34 min; total task time showed no difference (p≈0.65); groups were 7 tutorial, 6 Cody, 1 Cursor. | PLATEAU '25 (15th PLATEAU Workshop), Boston, July 2025: confirmed. Authors: Jiang, Koppel, Coblenz |
| 11 Nam et al. GILT | CONFIRMED | "user study with 32 participants"; "completing statistically significantly more sub-tasks (0.47 more, p < 0.01)"; "we did not find the utility gains in terms of time and understanding level"; "the impact of GILT was significant only in the Pros group model ... professionals completed 0.57 more sub-tasks ... students did not experience significant gains." | ICSE '24, Lisbon: confirmed |
| 12 METR | CONFIRMED | "16 developers with moderate AI experience complete 246 tasks"; "allowing AI actually increases completion time by 19%"; "After completing the study, developers estimate that allowing AI reduced completion time by 20%." (Their forecast before the study was 24%.) | arXiv 2507.09089, 2025; no venue stated |
| 13 Merino et al. | CONFIRMED | "study 181 of those [387 papers]"; "We found that 62% (i.e., 113) of the proposed software visualization approaches either do not include any evaluation, or include a weak evaluation (i.e., anecdotal evidence, usage scenarios)." | Journal of Systems and Software 144:165-180, Oct 2018: confirmed |
| 14 Feeling Easier, Working Harder | CONFIRMED (with nuance) | "20 novice CS students"; "raising tDRT reaction times by 11.2%, yet participants' perceived workload remained unchanged"; "no evidence that AI assistance improved comprehension at the difficulty levels evaluated; a ceiling effect limited power". Nuance: this is "no evidence of improvement" under a ceiling effect, not a shown absence of benefit. | arXiv comment: "Accepted to ICSE 2027" |
| 15 Stapleton et al. | CONFIRMED | "(n = 45)"; "participants performed significantly better (p = 0.029) using human-written summaries versus machine-generated summaries"; "participants' performance showed no correlation with the BLEU and ROUGE scores". (The body says "did not strongly correlate".) | ICPC '20 (28th ICPC, co-located with ICSE 2020): confirmed |
| 16 SWD-Bench | CONFIRMED | "In functionality detection, the best performance method achieves an average MCC of only 29.35."; "Human-written documentation remains competitive"; "H-Written ... outperform Chat and is even competitive with DocAgent". | arXiv 2604.06793; no venue stated |
| 17 CodeWiki | CONFIRMED | Table 1, Average row: "DeepWiki 64.06 ± 3.60 / CodeWiki 68.79 ± 3.84 +4.73"; abstract: "68.79% quality score with proprietary models, outperforming the closed-source DeepWiki baseline (64.06%)". | arXiv comment: "Accepted at ACL 2026" |
| 18 Tao et al. | CONFIRMED (with nuance) | "we received 180 responses – 99 from SDEs, 56 from SDETs, and 25 from PMs"; "it is difficult to acquire important information needs such as a change's completeness, consistency, and especially the risk"; "determining a change's risk is very important when understanding the change, but it is difficult". Nuance: the paper calls rationale "most importantly" needed; risk is high on importance and the hardest to get. | FSE '12 (SIGSOFT/FSE'12, Cary NC), DOI 10.1145/2393596.2393656: confirmed |
| 19 Same Scrutiny, More Time | CONFIRMED | "We recruited 32 software engineering practitioners"; "while the thoroughness of code review did not change for participants, they spent more time fixating on LLM-labelled code". | arXiv comment: "Accepted at ... ASE 2026" |
| 20 "Dias et al." change decomposition | WRONG (author) | Numbers and findings match: "28 software developers among professionals and graduate students"; "Change decomposition leads to fewer wrongly reported issues ... yet impacts neither understanding the change rationale nor the number of found defects."
